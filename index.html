Interactive Lab

Five real-time graphics demos related to my research, written from scratch in WebGL and running entirely in your browser.

click: select · drag: move / orbit · double-click: focus
Sampling

A physically based path tracer running in your GPU's fragment shaders. Every pixel traces light paths with up to 8 bounces through a Cornell box. Surfaces use a GGX microfacet BRDF with visible-normal sampling (Heitz 2018), and dielectrics refract with exact Fresnel. Direct light comes from next-event estimation combined with BSDF sampling by multiple importance sampling (Veach 1995); switch between BSDF, NEE and MIS and shrink the light to see why it matters. Samples accumulate progressively, and while you interact an edge-avoiding à-trous wavelet denoiser (Dammertz et al. 2010), guided by normal, depth and albedo buffers, cleans up the image. This is the same idea behind SVGF in real-time ray tracing. Click an object to select it and edit its material, drag it to move it, drag the background to orbit, and double-click to focus the thin-lens camera.

Lights

Direct lighting from hundreds of small neon lights, with exactly one shadow ray per pixel per frame and no denoiser, so all remaining noise is visible. Uniform sampling picks a random light, and most picks are far away or dim, so the alley turns into speckles. RIS (resampled importance sampling) draws M random candidates, keeps one in a tiny reservoir with probability proportional to its unshadowed contribution, and corrects its weight so the estimate stays unbiased. ReSTIR (Bitterli et al., SIGGRAPH 2020) goes further: each pixel merges its reservoir with last frame's reservoir at the reprojected position (temporal reuse) and then with a few neighbours' (spatial reuse), so every pixel draws on thousands of candidates for the cost of one shadow ray. Look at the wet asphalt and the walls between the signs: the noise drops sharply from left to right, while blinking signs and drifting lanterns show how the reservoirs follow lights that move. The reservoirs live in floating-point textures that are ping-ponged between frames.

An editable open world made of around 200,000 3D Gaussians, in the spirit of generative world models such as Genie 3 and World Labs' Marble. Describe a world or pick a preset. A keyword-driven procedural generator stands in for a learned model here: it reads the prompt (in English, Chinese or Japanese) and builds the terrain, lakes, forests, villages and weather. Everything is drawn with the 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline (Kerbl et al. 2023): EWA projection of each covariance, a depth sort in a Web Worker, and back-to-front alpha compositing. Unlike a frozen capture, the Gaussians stay live. They are relit on the GPU for any time of day, cast sun shadows through a splatted shadow map, sway in the wind and follow the seasons. Terrain Gaussians re-derive their height, normal, orientation and material from an editable heightfield every frame. You can sculpt and paint the land, place or erase trees, rocks, cabins and lanterns, flood the valley, and walk through it in first person (Walk, then WASD and drag to look). You can also open a real capture: Load .ply, or drop a file onto the view, shows any standard 3DGS .ply with plain 3DGS shading.

drag cloth to pull · drag background to orbit

A 40 × 40 cloth simulated with Extended Position-Based Dynamics (XPBD, Macklin et al. 2016) using many small substeps, the same family of solvers found in game engines, VR, and the physics simulators that generate training data for embodied agents and world models. Drag the cloth to pull it, switch to Cut and swipe to slice it, or stretch it hard until it tears. Drag empty space to orbit the camera.

move the cursor: it is your gaze

Eye-tracked foveated rendering, as used in headsets such as Apple Vision Pro and Meta Quest Pro. Visual acuity drops sharply outside the fovea (about 5° of the visual field), so the scene is ray-marched at full resolution only where you look (your cursor is the simulated gaze), at 1/2 and 1/4 resolution further out, and the layers are blended together. Turn on the overlay to see the shading-rate regions. When the cursor is idle, the gaze performs simulated saccades.
